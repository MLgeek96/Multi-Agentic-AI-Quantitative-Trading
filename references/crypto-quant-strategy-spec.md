## 1. Executive Summary & Core Philosophy

This specification details a production-grade quantitative trading framework designed for cryptocurrency markets. The strategy integrates structural chart pattern identification, momentum filtering, automated risk-reward validation, strict position sizing, and algorithmic execution safeguards derived from institutional market mechanics [15, 21].

### Core Entry Hierarchy Rule
The strategy enforces a strict **two-stage decision tree**:
1. **Primary Entry Hypothesis (Chart Patterns):** Structural price patterns (Reversals & Continuations) establish trade direction, price targets ($x$), and logical stop-loss anchor points [15, 16].
2. **Timing & Confirmation Filter (Momentum Indicators):** Technical indicators (RSI, Volume, MACD, MA Crossovers) must **never** be used in isolation to initiate positions. Instead, they act as timing gatekeepers to prevent entering setups where momentum is exhausted or diverging [18, 19, 20].

## 2. Dataset Architecture & Preprocessing

### 2.1 Market Data Requirements
* **Primary Timeframe (Structural Identification):** 1-hour ($1\text{h}$) and 4-hour ($4\text{h}$) OHLCV bars for identifying macro chart patterns and major trendlines [15, 16].
* **Execution Timeframe (Execution & 3 Price Points):** 15-minute ($15\text{m}$) OHLCV bars for fine-tuning entry, stop loss, and take profit levels [15, 16].
* **Data Schema:**
  `[Timestamp, Open, High, Low, Close, Volume, Quote_Volume, Trades_Count]`

### 2.2 Data Integrity & Crypto Market Mechanics
* **Continuous Trading ($24/7$):** Unlike traditional equity markets, crypto operates continuously. Gaps primarily occur during extreme volatility, liquidity drains, or exchange API downtime. Gaps are expected to fill over time unless accompanied by fundamental shifts [16, 17].
* **Volume Integrity:** Volume indicators require an authoritative central venue feed (e.g., primary liquid exchange consolidated book) to avoid false signals inherent in fragmented or OTC markets [19].
* **Spread Variance & Anomaly Detection:** Apply historical regression analysis on bid-ask spreads to establish dynamic variance thresholds, triggering alerts when spreads widen abnormally [3].

## 3. Pattern Recognition & Signal Engine

### 3.1 Reversal Patterns (Primary Signals)
Reversal patterns establish directional bias and explicit target move magnitudes ($x$) [16]:

| Pattern Type | Formation Logic | Target Calculation ($x$) [16] |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Head and Shoulders / Inverted** | Peak/Trough flanked by two lower/higher peaks/troughs; Breakout of Neckline. | Measure vertical distance $x$ from Head peak/trough to Neckline. Project $x$ from Breakout point. |
| **Double Top / Double Bottom ($W$-Shape)** | Two distinct price tests of major Resistance/Support level. | Measure vertical distance $x$ from Peak/Trough to Neckline. Project $x$ from Breakout point. |
| **Triple Top / Triple Bottom** | Three distinct price tests of major Resistance/Support level. | Measure vertical distance $x$ from Peak/Trough to Neckline. Project $x$ from Breakout point. |
| **Key Reversal Bar** | Extreme high/low bar closing near opposite extreme (engulfing range). | **No built-in target level.** System must automatically switch to shorter timeframe ($15\text{m}$) to locate local structural S/R for 3 price points [16]. |

### 3.2 Continuation Patterns
* **Triangles (Ascending / Descending):** Descending triangles feature falling resistance and horizontal support; Ascending triangles feature rising support and horizontal resistance. Breakout confirms trend continuation [16].
* **Flags & Pennants (Bullish / Bearish):** Brief consolidation periods following sharp directional moves. Breakout signals continuation of prior trend velocity [16].

## 4. Momentum Confirmation & Filter Matrix

An entry setup generated by Section 3 is **rejected** unless all enabled momentum confirmation gates pass [18, 20]:

```
                     [ Chart Pattern Breakout Detected ]
                                     │
                                     ▼
                      [ RSI Divergence Check Passed? ] ───(No)───► [ REJECT SIGNAL ]
                                     │ (Yes)
                                     ▼
                      [ Volume Confirmation Passed? ] ───(No)───► [ REJECT SIGNAL ]
                                     │ (Yes)
                                     ▼
                      [ MACD / MA Alignment Passed? ] ───(No)───► [ REJECT SIGNAL ]
                                     │ (Yes)
                                     ▼
                       [ EXECUTE POSITION MODULE ]
```

### 4.1 Filter Rules
1. **Volume Confirmation Gate:**
   * Breakout **must** be accompanied by above-average volume (e.g., $\text{Volume} > 1.5 \times \text{SMA}_{20}(\text{Volume})$).
   * Declining volume during a breakout indicates low market conviction and high risk of retracement/fakeout [18, 19].
2. **Relative Strength Index (RSI) Filter:**
   * **Overbought / Oversold Thresholds:** Standard range $70\text{--}80$ (Overbought) and $20\text{--}30$ (Oversold) [18].
   * **RSI Divergence Check:** If price achieves a Higher High while RSI forms a Lower High (or vice versa for shorts), a loss of momentum is flagged, invalidating long entries [18].
3. **MACD Alignment Engine:**
   * $\text{MACD Line} = \text{EWMA}(12) - \text{EWMA}(26)$ [19].
   * $\text{Signal Line} = \text{EWMA}(9, \text{MACD Line})$ [19].
   * Long entries require MACD Line > Signal Line and positive slope.
4. **Parameter Calibration:** Default parameters ($12, 26, 9$ for MACD; $14$ for RSI) must undergo **regression testing** against historical asset data to optimize win rate and profit expectancy [3, 20].

## 5. Risk Management & Position Sizing Framework

### 5.1 The Three Price Points Rule
Every trade requires pre-determined parameters before order submission [2, 15, 17]:
1. **Entry Price ($P_{\text{entry}}$):** Breakout price level confirmed by candle close or limit order retest.
2. **Take Profit Price ($P_{\text{target}}$):** Calculated from pattern target height $x$ or major technical resistance [16, 17].
3. **Stop Loss Price ($P_{\text{stop}}$):** Placed logically beyond technical support/resistance (e.g., below neckline/swing low) [1, 2, 17].

### 5.2 Risk:Reward Ratio Gate
* **Minimum Threshold:** The system strictly rejects any trade with a Risk:Reward ratio worse than $1:3$ [1, 7, 17]:
  $$\text{Risk:Reward Ratio} = \frac{|P_{\text{entry}} - P_{\text{stop}}|}{|P_{\text{target}} - P_{\text{entry}}|} \le \frac{1}{3}$$
* **Logical Placement Constraint:** Stop losses must be placed at technical market levels. Forcing a mechanical stop loss just to fit the $1:3$ ratio is prohibited as it causes premature stop-outs on noise [17].

### 5.3 Position Sizing Calculator (1% Risk Rule)
Capital allocation per trade is bounded by total account equity to prevent runaway account drawdowns [1, 2]:
* **Total Account Risk Capital:** $C_{\text{total}}$
* **Max Capital at Risk per Trade:** $R_{\text{trade}} = 0.01 \times C_{\text{total}}$ (1% Risk Rule)
* **Position Size Formula ($N_{\text{units}}$):**
  $$N_{\text{units}} = \frac{R_{\text{trade}}}{|P_{\text{entry}} - P_{\text{stop}}|}$$

### 5.4 Position Management & Trailing Stop Protocol
* **Golden Rule:** *"Never let a profitable position become a loss"* [2, 15].
* **Disciplined Execution:** *"He who trades without Stops, soon stops trading"* [2].
* **Step-based Trailing Stop:**
  * When price reaches $+1R$ profit ($\text{Gain} = |P_{\text{entry}} - P_{\text{stop}}|$), automatically move Stop Loss to Breakeven ($P_{\text{entry}}$).
  * When price reaches $+2R$ profit, adjust trailing stop to lock in a minimum of **33% retracement protection** relative to unrealized gains [7, 9].

## 6. Backtesting, System Safeguards & Log Audit Engine

### 6.1 Backtesting Methodology & Pitfalls
* **Look-Back Window Calibration:** Select a window long enough to prove endurance across multiple volatility regimes, but avoid ancient data that reflects obsolete market structures [10, 11].
* **Confirmatory Bias Warning:** Avoid over-optimizing parameters to fit historical noise ("Don't mistake a roasted duck for a golden goose") [10].
* **Observer Effect & Slippage:** Account for market impact—static backtest data does not reflect how dynamic live order placement shifts order books and causes execution slippage [12, 13, 21].
* **Stress Testing:** Perform stress tests against extreme tail events, liquidity shocks, and flash crashes [11, 21].

### 6.2 Production Safeguards & Execution Architecture
1. **Automated Kill-Switches:** Immediate strategy shutdown upon detecting unexpected API exceptions, connectivity drops, or drawdown limits [13, 14, 21].
2. **Order Tagging:** Every order sent to exchange matching engines must include a unique strategy reference tag (`order_reference`) to prevent order cross-contamination across algos [13, 21].
3. **Execution Log Auditing Engine:**
   Audit system logs continuously against exchange execution feeds (`Filled_bid_reference`, `Filled_ask_reference`, `action`, `traded_vol`) to verify order fills and resolve trade discrepancies [22]:
